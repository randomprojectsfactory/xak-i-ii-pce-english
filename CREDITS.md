# Credits and translation sources

## Existing English translations

The existing MSX English localisations are the foundation and reference material for this adaptation. Their original English translation credit remains with their authors.

### Xak I: The Art of Visual Stage

**Rieks Warendorp Torringa — English translation and English manuals, WOOMB.net, 2007.**

The WOOMB English manual also credits **Tobias Keizer** for the MSX English text routines and R800 speed mode, and **Arjan Bakker** for BitBuster compression. Those are credits for the MSX release; its MSX-specific executable routines are not the PCE text engine implemented here.

The translation credit and supporting roles are printed in the Staff section of the [WOOMB English manual, page 19 of the PDF](https://download.file-hunter.com/Manuals/Xak%201%20-%20English%20Manual.pdf#page=19). The [contemporary WOOMB release announcement](https://www.msx.org/news/msx-revival/en/xak-art-visual-stage-woombnet) provides additional release context.

### Xak II: Rising of the Redmoon

**Oasis — existing MSX English translation.**

The English reference used for this adaptation was the Oasis-labelled MSX v1.0 disk set. [Generation MSX records the translation under Oasis](https://generation-msx.nl/software/micro-cabin/xak-ii-rising-of-the-redmoon/translations/1386). The [MSX Translations archive](https://www.msxtranslations.com/archive.php) documents a separate set of later fixes; those fixes are not being presented as the authorship of the original English translation or as an MSX patch incorporated into the PCE executable.

## Work specific to this PCE adaptation

The project's contribution is the PC Engine implementation and adaptation: recovering and matching PCE script events to the MSX references, adapting wording where the versions differ, supplying translations for PCE-specific material, adding cinematic subtitles, localising native UI and graphical text, and implementing the English font, pagination and display corrections.

The PCE Japanese dialogue and audio control the meaning where the versions differ. Shared names and terminology are kept consistent, while PCE-specific details are retained. This is not a claim that the MSX scripts and the PCE edition match line for line, or that all English wording originated in this project.
